This analysis project investigates which Spotify audio features are most closely tied to song popularity, with the goal of giving producers, artists, labels, and streaming platforms data-driven insight into what shapes a track's success. Our team merged three Spotify-based datasets (large_set.csv, songs.csv, and spotify_songs.csv) with a Billboard Hot 100 dataset, which added a more stable "time on chart" measure of sustained popularity. After cleaning, standardizing features, and narrowing the data to 1,805 songs, they used twelve audio inputs (such as danceability, energy, loudness, valence, acousticness, and tempo) to predict popularity as a continuous value. They compared Random Forest, XGBoost, and CatBoost regression models, using 5-fold cross-validation and RandomizedSearchCV for tuning, and found that a tuned CatBoost model performed best, explaining roughly 73-76% of the variance in popularity with typical errors of about 7-9 points and minimal overfitting. We then concluded that the results only partially answered the business question, since popularity is also driven by non-audio factors like marketing, artist fame, and Spotify's recommendation algorithm, and they recommend incorporating that kind of data and refreshing the model with new data over time.
